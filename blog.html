THE LITTLE ART OF SAYING IT WELL

Write a greeting that
feels like you.

The most memorable wishes are rarely the most complicated. They sound like the person sending them, reflect the relationship, and mention a real reason the moment matters. These practical guides can help you start.

GUIDE 01 · PERSONALIZATION

How to write a personal greeting in three simple steps

Start with the person. Think of one thing you appreciate about them: their patience, humour, courage, generosity, or the way they make people feel welcome. A sentence that reflects a real quality often feels more meaningful than a grand compliment.

Connect it to the occasion. A birthday can be about growth and the year ahead. A wedding message can recognize teamwork and friendship, not only romance. A festival greeting can focus on peace, family, gratitude, renewal, or whatever makes the occasion meaningful to you.

Add a detail only you would know. Mention a shared memory, a small goal they are pursuing, or a tradition you enjoy together. You do not need to reveal private information; even a light reference such as “our late-night tea talks” can make a note feel personal.

Finish naturally. “Thinking of you,” “With love,” or a simple name can be enough. Read the message once before sharing and remove words you would never say out loud.

GUIDE 02 · LANGUAGE

English, Hindi or Hinglish: choose the language that feels natural

Choose the language your recipient is most comfortable reading, especially for older relatives or formal messages. Hindi can carry familiar expressions of warmth, blessings and respect. English may fit a professional greeting or a friend who usually communicates in English. Hinglish can feel conversational for people who mix Hindi and English in everyday chats.

There is no single correct choice. Use the greeting you would genuinely send to that person, not the language that looks most formal. Keep names spelled the way the recipient prefers, and check that Hindi text displays properly before downloading or sharing the final image.

For a mixed-language message, avoid switching languages just to sound trendy. Use it when it makes the thought clearer or more natural. A short sincere line usually works better than an overlong message filled with stock phrases.

GUIDE 03 · TONE

Pick a tone that suits the relationship

Heartfelt is useful when you want to express gratitude, care or affection. Elegant and formal works for colleagues, clients, teachers and acquaintances. Playful is best when you know the recipient enjoys humour—avoid jokes about age, appearance, relationships or personal struggles unless you are sure they will be welcomed. Devotional messages should be respectful and appropriate to the recipient’s beliefs.

For a professional message, be warm but specific. For a close friend, a private shared detail may feel more genuine than a polished quote. For someone going through a difficult time, acknowledge them without pressuring them to feel cheerful.

GUIDE 04 · PHOTOS & PRIVACY

Choose photos thoughtfully and share them with care

Use a photo you took yourself or have permission to share. Before adding someone else’s picture to a greeting, think about whether they would be comfortable with it being sent to a group or posted publicly. Avoid photos that reveal addresses, identity documents, school details, private conversations or other information the person may not want distributed.

In the current WishingASR creator, a selected picture is processed in your browser to create the card preview and PNG. The package does not include accounts or server-side photo storage. However, once you send or upload a finished card through another app or social network, that service’s own privacy terms apply. Do not treat a locally generated card as private after you share it.

GUIDE 05 · CULTURAL CONTEXT

Check festival dates and regional traditions

Festival dates can depend on lunar calendars, local observance, regional tradition and official announcements. WishingASR’s calendar is intended as a planning aid, not as an authoritative religious calendar. Confirm dates with a reliable local source before arranging travel, ceremonies or time off. For Islamic observances, dates may remain tentative until a moon sighting or local announcement.

When writing a festival greeting, keep it inclusive and respectful. If you are unsure how someone observes the occasion, a simple wish for peace, health and happiness is usually a thoughtful place to begin.

GUIDE 06 · CARD DESIGN

Make a card easy to read on a phone

Choose a design with enough contrast between text and background. Avoid long all-caps sentences and keep the main message to a few lines when possible. A recipient’s name, one strong thought and a simple sign-off can create a balanced card. When you add a photo, choose one with a clear subject and avoid placing the text over a busy area.

Preview the greeting at a small size before downloading. Open the PNG after it saves and check the Hindi characters, line breaks, names and photo crop. If you share the file through a messaging app, make sure it sends as an image rather than a compressed screenshot if quality matters.
